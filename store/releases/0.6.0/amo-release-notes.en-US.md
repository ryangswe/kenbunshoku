If card recognition finishes loading more than 10 seconds after the pointer last moved, while the pointer still rests over the video, Kenbunshoku looks once then, still within 60 seconds of that move, so a new tab whose model loads slowly can still name the card under a resting pointer. On a slower computer, the looks that keep a shown card fresh are spaced by what they cost there, and a card that arrives under a resting pointer can take up to about 4 seconds to show. While the pointer moves over a playing video, the next look still starts early enough that the card it names stays showing, unless a single look takes longer than that allows. While a look that got no answer waits to be tried again, no other look starts unless a pointer move or tap puts the pointer on a different card. Turning card hovers off and on in the popup after an idle unload starts them again at once if the main video is playing on a page where playing starts them; a tab in the background waits until you bring it to the front. After YouTube or Twitch moves to a page where card hovers do not run, a pointer resting over the small player no longer leads to a picture being read. On pages where playing starts card hovers, frames the page removes come back when the video next plays. Only the browser's own "video started playing" event loads card hovers, not one a page script makes up.
